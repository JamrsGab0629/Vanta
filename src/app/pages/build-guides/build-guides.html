Build Guide

Parts don't fail because they're bad — they fail because they don't fit each other. Here's what to check before you add anything to cart.

Before you buy, check these six

In order of how often they actually trip people up.

01

CPU socket vs motherboard socket

The CPU's socket has to match the motherboard's socket exactly. Not "close enough" — an LGA1700 chip will not physically seat in an AM5 board. Chipset (Z790, B650, etc.) affects features and overclocking, but socket is the hard gate.

Where to lookSpec sheet, "Socket" field
Common mismatchIntel LGA1700 vs AMD AM4/AM5
02

RAM generation vs motherboard

DDR4 and DDR5 sticks have different notch positions and voltages — they aren't interchangeable, even on boards from the same chipset family. Check the board's supported memory type before you look at speed or capacity.

Where to lookBoard spec, "Memory Type" field
Common mismatchDDR5 kit on a DDR4-only board
03

GPU length vs case clearance

High-end cards run long. Compare the card's listed length against the case's max GPU clearance, and against any drive cages or front fans that might sit in the way.

Where to lookGPU "Length (mm)", case "Max GPU Length"
Common mismatch300mm+ card in a compact mATX case
04

PSU wattage headroom

Add up the CPU and GPU's rated power draw, then leave roughly 30% headroom above that total for the rest of the system and to keep the PSU out of its least efficient range under load.

Where to lookCPU/GPU "TDP" or "Power Draw" fields
Common mismatch550W PSU paired with a 570W+ flagship GPU
05

PSU connector vs GPU power input

Newer high-wattage cards expect a 12V-2x6 (or older 12VHPWR) connector, not just an 8-pin. A high-wattage PSU without the right cable still means an adapter or a compatibility gap.

Where to lookGPU "Power Connector" field, PSU included cables
Common mismatchFlagship GPU, PSU only ships 8-pin cables
06

Storage interface vs motherboard slots

NVMe M.2 drives need an M.2 slot, and boards vary in how many they have and whether a populated slot disables a SATA port. SATA SSDs need a free SATA port and cable regardless.

Where to lookBoard manual, "Storage" section
Common mismatchSecond NVMe drive, board only has one M.2 slot

Quick pre-checkout pass

Run through this in order before you finalize a build.

1
Sockets matchCPU socket = motherboard socket, exactly.
2
RAM type matchesDDR4 or DDR5, whichever the board actually takes.
3
GPU fits the caseCard length is under the case's max clearance.
4
PSU has enough headroomWattage covers CPU + GPU draw with room to spare.
5
PSU has the right cablesConnector type matches what the GPU actually needs.
6
Storage slots are freeEnough M.2 or SATA ports for every drive in the build.
